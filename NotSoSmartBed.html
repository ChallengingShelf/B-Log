He’s got his bed in the clouds

Themes: IoT | Cloud | Outages | Infrastructure Dependence | Sleeping | Eggs in 1 basket

HamiltonHacks November 2026

Ur bed got hacked. Kinda. Same effect as a DDOS I suppose.

There’s been a lot of widespread outages reported in mainstream media recently and it’s reminded me of an outage a couple years ago that had some real world impact, in the bedroom.

Have you seen the tv series black mirror? I think there’s 7 seasons now, it’s great! it’s dystopian, not-so-distant-futuristic, tech themed. Every day we inch closer and closer to living in a real life black mirror episode.

What happened?

Back in October 2025, there was a major outage on AWS US-East-1, infrastructure which has its physical location based in North Virginia *factcheck, if I’m not mistaken.

Many many many many internet services are hosted here on these Amazon servers. A big chunk of the internet was broke because of a timing bug / race condition which erased the DNS Address of the server itself (wordy bit —- there was an automatic updater to keep the address of it updated and there was a backup one both running so it would keep working if one failed, one of them deleted the true address and no one could find it for a while and it was back to normal in about 15 hours). 15 hours! Imagine the legal compensation claims. Anyway, OutageCost.com/case-studies/aws-october-2025 covers this and explains it better than I can to be honest.

these things happen. The cloud is not up in the sky, it’s a big dirty building in the middle of nowhere somewhere. If something happens to this building or the electric black boxes inside it, something happens to your cloud dependent services. Obviously. But the point is, the service, and the building isn’t invincible and shit happens.

Due to the many services using this specific cloud the impact of this was pretty widespread and global.

  • Snapchat app was down for most users
  • Fortnite and Roblox had login and matchmaking issues
  • Coinbase and Robin Hood and account access disrupted
  • Amazons Ring doorbell was dead (maybe that’s a good thing) and Alexa too. Etc etc, the impact was massive.
  • Perplexity Ai

However, my personal favourite example, is from EightSleep a luxury bed company,

Premium quality internet connected beds that rely on temperature control and bed positioning/ elevation via the cloud. Flying cars, they said.

The smart home is the future! So when this outage happened, the bed wouldn’t respond to its usual commands and in fact would be stuck on its current setting whatever that would be. So the bed unresponsive would be stuck on its settings.

Some beds stuck in awkward positions and in some cases beds overheated to 110° F, now that’s actually dangerous.

Can you imagine, your bed is connected to the internet. Akin to a WiFi enabled kettle/ microwave.

In theory, and please picture this image in your mind, people could have been stuck in bed, mid-movement of the bed for example a V shape. Quite funny really, no deaths reported to my knowledge. It’s just a good example about IoT and cloud risk, I just like the nature of this case study, and it’s actually real. Hilarious.

Lessons learned?

I don’t know really, it’s quite subjective this one. Yes, IoT is designed to make things easier to make lives marginally better, built for convenience and solving micro problems. And yes a remote control bed is a nice idea, you’re on the commute home and you turn on your WiFi enabled kettle, you switch the lights on remotely and you move your bed to its favourite setting so when you lie down, it’s exactly how as comfortable as you want. Great.

But another angle is always that there are people that need this, that rely on this for their health conditions or disability. This should always be considered.

It’s yet another example to chuck on the pile of reliance on infrastructure wake up calls and redundancies. If the whole internet is on a few boxes in 1 warehouse how high is that risk? And are people aware? The bed company added and “outage” mode to the beds and I think everyone lived happily ever after. Maybe a bit of sue-ing over in the US, obviously.

Otherwise, in this day and age, if you can help it, do you really want your bed, your place of sleep, connected to the big bad internet? I don’t.
